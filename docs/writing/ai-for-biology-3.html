AI for Biology #3:
Can AI Design Better Proteins?

How protein language models and agentic AI systems are changing the future of protein engineering and drug discovery.

July 2026 · Protein Design · Foundation Models · Drug Discovery

The Challenge of Protein Engineering

For decades, protein engineering has followed a familiar cycle:

Design → Build → Test → Repeat

Researchers propose a protein sequence, produce it experimentally, measure its properties and use the results to improve the next design.

This approach has produced remarkable breakthroughs, but it is slow, expensive and limited by the number of experiments that can reasonably be performed.

The Protein Design Space Problem

The challenge comes from the enormous number of possible protein sequences.

Even relatively small proteins can have an astronomical number of possible combinations of amino acids. Searching this space experimentally is impossible.

This is where AI begins to change the paradigm.

Learning the Language of Proteins

Protein language models apply ideas originally developed for natural language processing to biological sequences.

Instead of learning relationships between words, these models learn patterns within amino acid sequences.

  • How evolution has shaped protein sequences
  • Relationships between amino acids
  • Patterns associated with structure and function
  • Representations useful for downstream biological tasks

By learning these patterns, AI models can generate new protein sequences, predict the effects of mutations and identify promising candidates for experimental testing.

Generating Proteins Is Not Enough

However, designing a protein is not simply a matter of generating a sequence.

A successful protein needs to satisfy many biological constraints:

  • Correct folding and structural stability
  • Desired biological function
  • Specificity for the intended target
  • Manufacturability
  • Safety and compatibility

A model that produces plausible sequences is only one component of a much larger scientific process.

Towards Agentic Protein Engineering

The future of AI-driven protein design will likely not be a single model receiving a prompt and producing a final answer.

Instead, it may look like an ecosystem of specialized models and agents collaborating throughout the discovery workflow.

  • 🧬 Generate candidate protein sequences
  • 🧪 Predict structure and biological properties
  • 📊 Evaluate biochemical characteristics
  • 📚 Search scientific literature and previous experiments
  • 🔄 Refine designs based on computational feedback
  • 👩‍🔬 Keep scientists involved in the decision process

The Role of Human Expertise

AI will not remove the need for protein engineers and biological scientists.

Instead, the opportunity is to allow researchers to explore a design space that is far too large for humans alone.

The strongest systems will combine:

Human expertise + Foundation models + Agentic AI systems

The Future of Protein Design

The next generation of biological breakthroughs may come from systems that combine machine learning with experimental science.

From therapeutic proteins to industrial enzymes and synthetic biology applications, AI could accelerate the transition from hypothesis to validated biological function.

The goal is not replacing scientists. The goal is expanding what scientists can explore.

Open Questions

  • How can AI models better connect sequence, structure and function?
  • How can computational predictions be efficiently validated experimentally?
  • Can autonomous scientific agents accelerate protein engineering?
  • Which applications will benefit most: enzymes, antibodies or therapeutics?
