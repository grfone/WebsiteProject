AI for Biology #1:
From LLMs to Agentic AI

How scientific AI systems could evolve from answering questions to actively supporting the research process.

July 2026 · Agentic AI · Computational Biology · Drug Discovery

Beyond the LLM

Large language models have demonstrated remarkable abilities in understanding and generating human language. They can summarize papers, answer questions, write code and assist researchers in many different tasks.

However, the most interesting question is not only:

"What happens when language models become larger?"

The more interesting question is:

"What happens when a language model becomes part of an intelligent system capable of interacting with scientific tools and workflows?"

From Models to Scientific Agents

A researcher does not usually solve a problem by asking a single question and receiving a final answer. Scientific discovery is an iterative process involving:

  • Reading and understanding previous research
  • Designing computational experiments
  • Analyzing biological datasets
  • Interpreting results
  • Generating new hypotheses
  • Planning the next experiment

An agentic AI system aims to support this workflow by connecting different capabilities into a coordinated process.

A Scientific Research Assistant

Imagine an AI assistant capable of:

  • 🧬 Searching and analyzing scientific literature about a target protein
  • 📊 Processing and interpreting omics datasets
  • 🐍 Writing and executing Python analysis workflows
  • 📈 Evaluating results and identifying patterns
  • 🔄 Deciding which analysis should be performed next
  • 📚 Returning to the literature when uncertainty remains

None of these individual capabilities are completely new. The important innovation comes from connecting them into a system that can support complex scientific reasoning.

Why This Matters for Biology

Modern biology is producing increasingly large amounts of data: genomic sequences, protein structures, microscopy images, molecular measurements and scientific publications.

The challenge is no longer only generating data. The challenge is navigating the enormous number of reasoning steps between a biological hypothesis and a validated discovery.

In areas such as synthetic biology and drug discovery, AI systems that can help researchers explore this complexity could become powerful scientific collaborators.

The Future of AI for Life Sciences

I believe the next generation of AI for biology will not be defined by a single increasingly capable model.

Instead, it will emerge from ecosystems of specialized agents working together:

Literature Agent
Data Analysis Agent
Coding Agent
Reasoning Agent
Scientific Assistant

The goal is not replacing scientists. The goal is extending human scientific capability.

Final Thoughts

Building better models remains essential. But building better systems around those models may become equally important.

The future of scientific AI may not be a single artificial intelligence solving biology. It may be a collaboration between humans and networks of specialized AI agents accelerating discovery.
